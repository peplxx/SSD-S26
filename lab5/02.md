# Observability

> <https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Observability_(software)>

Observability is all about monitoring and analyzing telemetry [signals](https://opentelemetry.io/docs/concepts/signals/) to catch and troubleshoot *problems*:

- **Infrastructure/Cloud monitoring**: catch hardware failures and track resource usage/limits.
- **Service monitoring**: catch software bugs and ensure reliability/performance.
- **Security monitoring**: catch intrusions and ensure system security.

<details>
<summary>Example telemetry data and relevant alerts:</summary>
<ul>
<li><b>Response times</b>: alerts on high latency.</li>
<li><b>Resource utilization</b>: alerts on approaching limits.</li>
<li><b>Error rate</b>: alerts on high rates of error responses.</li>
<li><b>Server health</b>: alerts on detected downtime.</li>
<li><b>Application logs</b>: alerts on large volumes or presence of particular lines.</li>
<li><b>Test coverage</b>: alerts on failures in continuous testing processes.</li>
<li><b>Vulnerability catalog</b>: alerts on a known CVE affecting a used component.</li>
</ul>
</details>

## Observability Pipeline

Three steps are generally performed to achieve observability:

1. **Data Collection**: telemetry data is obtained from different targets (using a push/pull model).

    - OpenTelemetry Collector, Prometheus Exporter, Promtail (superseded by GrafanaLabs Alloy), FluentBit/Fluentd, FileBeat/Logstash, Zabbix Agent, and vector.dev
1. **Data Storage**: data is parsed, processed, stored, and indexed in a database that is optimized for searching/querying.
    - Prometheus TSDB, InfluxDB, GrafanaLabs Loki/Tempo/Mimir, and Apache Lucene (the search engine used by ElasticSearch and OpenSearch)
1. **Data Visualization**: data is collected from different stores and summarized/abstracted/presented in visualization dashboards.
    - Grafana, Kibana, OpenSearch Dashboards, Zabbix Web Interface.

## Tool Sprawl

Observability requires the integration between different systems, presenting the need for multiple specialized tools (to support one or more steps from the pipeline explained above).

Tools that are commonly used together are collectively known as an observability/monitoring *stack* and given fancy abbreviations (e.g., ELK, PLG, LGTM, TICK).
